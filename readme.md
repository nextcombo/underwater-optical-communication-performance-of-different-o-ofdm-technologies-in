# Underwater Optical Communication: Performance of Different O-OFDM Technologies in Visibility Communication under Weak Turbulence

Introduction to Underwater (UW) Wireless Communication refers to the transmission of digital data over underwater wireless channels using radio frequency (RF), sound waves, or light waves as carriers. In some cases, such as temporary experiments, wire breaks, and long-distance experiments, it is not feasible to transmit data through underwater cables. Therefore, UW wireless communication is necessary for these scenarios. Additionally, with the rapid increase in UW services such as marine biology research, offshore oil drilling, submarine exploration, and monitoring, there is a need for reliable and high-data-rate UW wireless communication links. Some advantages of UW communication include:
· UW wireless communication can be used to provide early warning of tsunami generated by earthquakes on the seabed,
· It avoids data spoofing and privacy leakage,
· It has advantages in pollution monitoring.
There are three options for implementing UW communication: acoustic, RF, and optical. i) Acoustic UWC: This is the most widely used and mature technology for UW wireless communication. The range of acoustic links is several kilometers, significantly longer than that of RF and optical links [1]. Low data rates (in units of kbps), severe communication delays (in units of seconds), and expensive and energy-consuming transceivers are some limitations of this UWC method. ii) RF UWC: It has the advantage of smoothly transitioning through the air/water interface. Moreover, compared to optical and acoustic communication, RF signals can withstand greater turbulence [3]. However, its supported link range is shorter (up to a few meters), and its transceivers are also expensive and energy-consuming. iii) UW Optical Communication or Visibility Light Communication (VLC): UW Optical Communication has the highest transmission data rate (in Gbps), high bandwidth, lowest link delay, and lowest implementation cost among the three methods. This method can also be used for real-time data transmission applications. UW Optical Communication also offers higher security compared to RF and acoustic communication. Some limitations of waterborne optical communication include large absorption and scattering of underwater optical signals, requiring precise alignment conditions, and the need for reliable underwater equipment.
Underwater Sensor Networks (UWSNs) are designed to meet the growing demands of marine exploration. Submarine sensors, relay floats, autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs), and remotely operated vehicles (ROVs) are all components of UW sensor networks. These nodes can perform sensing, processing, and communication duties to jointly monitor the UW environment. These ultra-wideband sensor networks have had a significant impact on ultra-wideband optical communication. In these systems, light sources such as LEDs and laser diodes are used.

Conclusion This paper investigates the bit error performance of UVLC systems in weak marine stations under spatial variations using M-QAM DCO-OFDM and EGC. We have discovered an accurate theoretical model for the bit error rate of M-QAM DCO-OFDM systems under weak disorder conditions. We used a Monte Carlo ray-tracing system to simulate various orders of route losses and power responses. Modeling and comparative analysis were conducted on the bit error rates for various orders. The results indicate that high-diversity systems can effectively mitigate the effects of chaos, with the diversity gain increasing as the intensity of chaos increases. Analyzing under weak turbulence conditions, ACO-OFDM outperforms DCO-OFDM in underwater communication.
